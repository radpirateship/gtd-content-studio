Traditional Finnish saunas for the home

Traditional saunas heat the air with a rock-topped electric or wood-fired stove, so you get the 165–195°F dry heat and the steam burst that infrared cannot reproduce. Every unit we carry is built from kiln-dried clear cedar, hemlock or thermally modified aspen, ships freight to your driveway, and qualifies for HSA/FSA reimbursement with a letter of medical necessity.

Most buyers land here after comparing traditional against infrared. If you want the highest heat, the löyly steam ritual, and a cabin that will still be standing in twenty years, this is your category. If you want a lower-temperature session you can run daily on a standard outlet, start with infrared instead.

Buying guide

How to choose in three decisions

Answer these in order and the shortlist writes itself. Everything else — wood species, lighting, sound — is preference.

01

Where will it live

Indoors — needs 4" clearance on all sides and a level, sealed floor. Basements are ideal.

Outdoors — needs a pad or deck rated for 1,200–2,600 lb and a weather-rated shell.

02

How many bathers

2–3 person — one bench, 4.5–6kW heater, fits a 5×7 footprint.

4–8 person — two tiers, 8–9kW, and a dedicated 40–50A circuit.

03

Electric or wood

Electric — 30–45 minute preheat, timer and app control, needs an electrician.

Wood-fired — off-grid, hotter, requires a chimney and local burn approval.

Questions

Traditional sauna FAQ

Do I need a permit to install a sauna at home?–
Indoor saunas rarely need a building permit, but the 240V circuit almost always needs an electrical permit and inspection. Outdoor cabins over a certain footprint can count as an accessory structure in some municipalities — call your local building department before the pad goes in.
What does a traditional sauna cost to run?+
A 6kW heater drawing full power for a 45-minute preheat plus a 30-minute session uses roughly 5–6 kWh. At the US average of about 17¢ per kWh that is under $1 a session, and less once the cabin is up to temperature.
Can I use HSA or FSA funds?+
Yes, with a letter of medical necessity from your provider. We accept HSA and FSA cards at checkout and can supply an itemized receipt coded for reimbursement. Talk to your plan administrator first — coverage varies.
How long does a sauna last outdoors?+
Twenty years or more for clear cedar or thermally modified wood on a proper base, with a UV oil every two seasons. The heater is the wear item — most electric elements run 8–12 years of regular use.
